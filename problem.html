The problem is visualization

Nipple-sparing mastectomy is the gold standard of mastectomy, preserving the skin envelope and nipple-areola complex for superior aesthetic and psychological outcomes. It requires excising the broad, shallow breast gland through an incision of roughly 10–12 cm, navigated largely by direct line of sight. No current tool solves visualization, ergonomics, and tissue-handling challenges while preserving the open NSM technique, incision, dissection plane, and haptics surgeons already trust.

Limited Patient candidacy, constrained by breast size, oncologic disposition, and ptosis
5–14% Skin flap necrosis following mastectomy with reconstruction, more severe in NSM
>50% Surgeons reporting career-affecting musculoskeletal pain

Visualization difficulty leads to:

Ergonomic surgeon pain

Seeing the working plane demands non-anatomic posture — neck extension, back flexion, sustained shoulder elevation. The cumulative toll drives chronic injury and early attrition.

Longer surgery

Holding a usable view requires constant repositioning of the table, the surgeon, and the retractor. Surgeons describe this as a meaningful time cost.

Retraction trauma

An assistant who cannot see the plane holds high, sustained tension on the flap. That loading is a recognized contributor to necrosis and reoperation.

Sources: Matsen et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2016 (necrosis rates); Epstein et al., JAMA Surg 2018 and Davis et al., J Surg Res 2014 (musculoskeletal injury); Hallbeck et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2020 (NSM workload). Operative-time observations reflect Revelascope customer discovery interviews with practicing breast surgeons.
