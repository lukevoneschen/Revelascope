The Problem

The problem is visualization

Nipple-sparing mastectomy is the gold standard of mastectomy, preserving the skin envelope and nipple-areola complex for superior aesthetic and psychological outcomes. It also requires excising a broad, shallow gland through an incision of roughly 10–12 cm, navigated largely by direct line of sight. Everything difficult about the operation follows from that narrow visual corridor.

Gold standard NSM is the cosmetic gold standard in oncologic breast surgery
5–14% Skin flap necrosis following mastectomy with reconstruction, with nipple-sparing mastectomy associated with more severe necrosis
>50% Surgeons reporting career-affecting musculoskeletal pain

Visualization difficulty leads to:

Ergonomic surgeon pain

To see the working plane, surgeons adopt non-anatomic postures — neck extension, back flexion, twisting, sustained shoulder elevation. Workload studies find NSM more demanding than skin-sparing mastectomy. The cumulative toll contributes to chronic injury and the early loss of experienced surgeons.

Longer surgery

Maintaining a usable view requires constant repositioning: raising and lowering the table, moving to the opposite side, pausing to re-establish line of sight. Breast surgeons describe this repositioning as a meaningful time cost, and some report needing breaks mid-procedure from physical fatigue.

Retraction trauma

When the assistant cannot see the dissection plane, they hold high, sustained tension on the mastectomy flap to keep the corridor open. That mechanical loading is a recognized contributor to nipple and skin-flap necrosis, driving reoperation and compromising reconstruction.

Sources: Matsen et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2016 (necrosis rates); Epstein et al., JAMA Surg 2018 and Davis et al., J Surg Res 2014 (musculoskeletal injury); Hallbeck et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2020 (NSM workload). Operative-time and fatigue observations reflect Revelascope customer discovery interviews with practicing breast surgeons.
