The problem is visualization

Nipple-sparing mastectomy is performed through a small incision, navigated largely by direct line of sight. When the surgeon can't see the plane, three problems follow.

Retraction trauma 5–14% skin-flap necrosis Tap to flip ↻

An assistant who can't see the plane holds sustained tension on the flap — a recognized driver of necrosis and reoperation.

Surgeon injury >50% report career-affecting pain Tap to flip ↻

Seeing the plane forces non-anatomic posture — neck extension, back flexion, elevated shoulders. The cumulative toll drives chronic injury and early attrition.

Longer operations Constant repositioning to see Tap to flip ↻

Holding a usable view means constantly repositioning the table, surgeon, and retractor — a meaningful time cost surgeons feel every case.

Sources: Matsen et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2016; Epstein et al., JAMA Surg 2018; Davis et al., J Surg Res 2014; Hallbeck et al., Ann Surg Oncol 2020. Operative-time observations from Revelascope customer discovery interviews.
