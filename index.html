A retractor built around what the surgeon can't see.

Revelascope integrates a camera and LED illumination directly into a surgical retractor blade, giving the operating surgeon a visual field that traditional retraction can't provide — developed first for nipple-sparing mastectomy.

The problem

In nipple-sparing mastectomy, visualization difficulty during flap dissection is close to universal among the surgeons we've interviewed — it's the pain point that comes up first and most consistently, ahead of ergonomics or training gaps.

Poor visibility drives aggressive retraction, and aggressive retraction drives flap and nipple trauma. Published rates of nipple necrosis following NSM range from roughly 5–30% (Matsen et al., Annals of Surgical Oncology, 2016) — a wide enough range to suggest technique and visualization, not just patient selection, play a real role. Patients with larger or more ptotic breasts are frequently excluded from NSM altogether because that ischemic risk is compounded by exactly this visibility problem.

The approach

Integrated imaging

A camera and LED illumination are built into the retractor blade itself, referenced to a fixed geometry rather than held or aimed separately — giving a consistent view of the dissection plane without a second instrument or a second set of hands.

Single-use by design

Revelascope is a fully disposable device — no reprocessing, no sterilization cycle to manage, and a design that can move at the pace of the OR it's built for.

Status
Investigational device — in development. Provisional patent filed. Not for sale.
